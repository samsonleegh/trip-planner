Quiet Bali Loop

Five days for three people, keeping well away from the south coast and Ubud. The loop climbs into the rice terraces and the misty lake country around Munduk, drops to the north coast for waterfalls and a hot spring, then runs east to snorkel off Amed and walk the Sidemen valley. It ends with a market town and a babi guling lunch on the way to the airport.

Your questions

Short answers first
How off-track is this? Mostly villages, lakes and back roads, with one well-known stop.
Nature 55%Villages & markets 20%Road 25%

Jatiluwih and Sekumpul are on most north Bali itineraries, but you're there for the long trails rather than the viewpoints. Everything else is mostly local visitors: a sacred lake you can only paddle, village waterfall paths, a walled Bali Aga village, the Sidemen rice valley and Klungkung's cloth market. What we left out ↓

Canyoning or caving? Canyoning, yes, as a swap on Day 3. Skip the caves.

North Bali's canyons (Gitgit, Sambangan, Kalimudah, Kerenkali) are 45 minutes from Munduk, and operators run them all year with morning starts. It costs about US$90–175 per person, so it replaces Sekumpul that day rather than adding to it. Details on Day 3 ↓

Bali has no real caving to speak of. Tukad Cepung now has photo queues, and its light beams are unlikely in December. Goa Lawah is a bat cave you only see from the temple steps. Neither is worth the detour.

Day by day

Times are WITA (UTC+8) · sunrise ≈ 05:45
Sat12 DecDay 1

Up into the rice bowl

Airport → Jatiluwih → Munduk, about 100 km. Two nights in the hills.

Rice terracesTrail walkLocal lunch
  1. Arrive

    Driver meets you at Ngurah Rai (DPS). Head straight north and out of the south's traffic.

  2. +2 h

    North through Tabanan. Stop for lunch in Tabanan town: ayam betutu (slow-cooked spiced chicken) or nasi campur at a warung your driver likes.

  3. 13:30

    Jatiluwih, the UNESCO-listed subak terraces. Skip the roadside café strip and walk the blue route (3.1 km) or the yellow route (2.3 km) out among the paddies and irrigation channels. Entry ≈ IDR 75–100k.

  4. +1.5 h

    Over the Bedugul pass and along the ridge above Lakes Buyan and Tamblingan to Munduk. Evenings are cool, about 18 °C.

  5. 18:30

    Dinner in Munduk village. Clove and coffee country, so try the local coffee.

If it's pouring

The terraces still look good in drizzle, but the trails get slick in a downpour. Plan B is a soak at Penatahan hot spring, 20 min south of Jatiluwih (≈ IDR 50k).

Heads up

In Dec 2025 farmers protested a crackdown on illegal buildings here, and visitor numbers dropped for weeks. It's back to normal in 2026, but a new central car park is due by end-2026, so expect some roadworks.

Sun13 DecDay 2

Lake, forest, falls, hot spring

Munduk on foot, then down to the north coast for the afternoon.

Dawn canoeLake templesWaterfall trekHot spring
  1. 06:00

    Lake Tamblingan at dawn by wooden paddle boat (no engines allowed). Each boat takes two passengers plus the rower, so book two boats for three people (≈ IDR 150–200k per boat for 30 min, negotiable). You'll paddle past lakeside temples, including Pura Ulun Danu Tamblingan and Pura Dalem Tamblingan. Lake entry ≈ IDR 20–30k. Bring a sarong, and don't swim: the lake is sacred.

  2. 08:30

    Breakfast back in Munduk.

  3. 09:30

    Walk from the village to Labuhan Kebo and Melanting falls through clove and coffee gardens. Allow about 2.5 h, ≈ IDR 20k each. Add Red Coral (Munduk) falls if your legs are good (≈ IDR 45k).

  4. 13:00

    Lunch in Munduk.

  5. +1 h

    Down the switchbacks to the north coast near Seririt.

  6. 15:00

    Air Panas Banjar: tiered sulphur pools fed by carved stone spouts in a jungle garden, used mostly by locals. ≈ IDR 45k, cash only (there's no ATM), open until about 17:30. Then back up to Munduk for dinner.

Munduk in December

This is the wettest corner of Bali, with about 20 rain days in a normal December. This year should be drier (see weather), but the paths will still be muddy, so wear trainers with grip.

Mon14 DecDay 3

Waterfall morning, coast road east

Two big waterfalls, then the dry north-east coast to Amed.

Waterfall swimGorge trekCoast road
  1. 07:30

    Check out. Twenty minutes to Wanagiri.

  2. 08:00

    Banyumala twin falls at opening time, before anyone else. It's 25 min down to a pool you can swim in. ≈ IDR 50k.

  3. +1 h

    Via Gitgit to Sekumpul.

  4. 10:30

    Sekumpul, seven falls in one gorge. The basic ticket (≈ IDR 20k) gets you the viewpoints. Going down to the base is 350+ steps and 2–3 h round trip. Touts say a guide is compulsory. It isn't for the main base, but a guided trek (≈ IDR 125–200k pp) is worth it to cross the river to the far falls. Pay only at the official counter.

  5. 13:30

    Lunch at a warung in Sekumpul village.

  6. +2.5 h

    East along the coast road through Tejakula. It's drier here, with fewer villas and lontar palms. Optional stop: Les waterfall, 15–20 min each way up a stream (≈ IDR 10–30k).

  7. 17:30

    Check in at Amed. Watch the sunset from the Jemeluk viewpoint, then have grilled fish on the beach.

Canyoning swap

Instead of Banyumala and Sekumpul, spend the morning canyoning: jumps, abseils and natural slides down a river gorge. Rindu Adventures picks up from Munduk (Gitgit canyon ≈ IDR 1.4M pp, Sambangan ≈ 1.55M pp). Adventure & Spirit is ICOpro-certified (Kalimudah US$145, Kerenkali US$175 pp). Take a morning slot, and drive on to Amed afterwards. Trips are called off if the river is too high, so get the refund terms in writing.

Tue15 DecDay 4

Reef at dawn, a walled village, the quiet valley

Amed → Tenganan → Sidemen, about 70 km.

SnorkelShipwreckBali Aga villageRice valley
  1. 06:30

    Snorkel off the beach while the water is calmest (until about 10:30). The Japanese wreck at Banyuning lies a few metres down, and Jemeluk has a coral wall. Gear ≈ IDR 50–100k.

  2. 09:30

    Breakfast and check out.

  3. 10:30

    Optional: Tirta Gangga water palace, 30 min away (≈ IDR 90k). It's popular, so keep it short.

  4. 12:00

    Tenganan Pegringsingan, one of the last walled Bali Aga villages, older than the island's Hindu-Javanese culture. Families here still weave geringsing double-ikat, which takes years per cloth. Entry is by donation; sign the guest book.

  5. 13:30

    Lunch nearby in Candidasa.

  6. +1 h

    Inland to Sidemen.

  7. 15:30

    Check in above the rice terraces. Sidemen is a weaving village too: look for songket looms in front yards. Dinner with the valley view.

Sea check

After a night of heavy rain, visibility at Amed drops from about 20 m to 5–10 m. If so, skip the snorkel and give Tenganan more time.

Wed16 DecDay 5

Rice valley at dawn, a market town, babi guling

Sidemen → Klungkung → airport, about 70 km.

Valley walkRoyal courtCloth marketBabi guling
  1. 06:30

    Sidemen rice-field walk with a local guide, about 2 h along the terraces and down to the Unda river. On a clear morning you'll see Agung from below. Your hotel can arrange a guide (≈ IDR 300k for the group).

  2. 09:30

    Breakfast and check out.

  3. 10:30

    Kertha Gosa in Klungkung (Semarapura), the old royal court of justice. Its ceiling is painted with heaven-and-hell panels in the Kamasan style. ≈ IDR 50k, sarong and museum included.

  4. 11:15

    Pasar Semarapura across the road, east Bali's main market for endek and songket cloth. Parts of it are still under renovation.

  5. 12:30

    Lunch: babi guling (spit-roast pork) at Mek Subur in Banjarangkan, a true local spot with a garlic-heavy rub. Or try Pande Egi in Beng, Gianyar.

  6. +1.5 h

    To the airport. Aim for a flight after about 16:00.

Our call

  • Book one driver for all 5 days and send them this route. Ask for an English speaker, and agree in writing on the daily hour cap, fuel, tolls and where the driver sleeps.
  • December is peak season and prices run 20–30% higher. North and east routes cost extra, and 06:00 pickups add about IDR 100k. All of that is in the range above.
  • Self-driving isn't worth it here. The roads to Munduk and Sidemen are narrow and steep, with trucks on the Gitgit bends.

Where to save or splurge

  • Swap Wapa for a Sidemen guesthouse and you save roughly US$150 on the night.
  • Canyoning adds about US$90–175 per person on Day 3.
  • The guided Sekumpul trek is the one guide that's worth paying for.

All prices are from 2025–26 listings and news, and some sources disagree. Bring cash in small notes. Most village sites don't take cards.

Before you go

Ticks stay in this browser only

Left out on purpose

Ubud, Tegallalang, Canggu, Seminyak, Kuta: the crowds you asked to avoid. Lempuyang Gate: hour-long photo queues. Batur sunrise: hundreds of hikers on one path, and a compulsory guide-association guide. Agung: you've climbed it. Trunyan: ongoing reports of boatmen demanding extra money mid-lake. Penglipuran: a polished show village. Tukad Cepung: photo queues, and light beams unlikely in December. Nusa Penida and West Bali / Menjangan: each needs its own 2 days.

Weather this December

Bali's weather office says a very strong El Niño is delaying the wet season. Most of Bali only starts getting rain in December, and the peak comes in Jan–Feb 2027. So expect it to be drier than a normal December, especially on the north and east coasts. Munduk's highlands stay the wettest and foggiest part of the route, which is why every outdoor stop is in the morning. Nothing big is on the Balinese calendar for 12–16 Dec (Galungan is 13 Jan 2027), though a village temple festival may pop up anywhere.
